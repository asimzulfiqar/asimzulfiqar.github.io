Everyone talks about the flexibility of freelancing. Work from anywhere! Set your own hours! Be your own boss!

What they don't mention: You're also your own accountant, sales team, customer support, and IT department.

After 230+ IoT projects as a freelancer, here's what I wish someone had told me at the beginning:

**1. "Flexible hours" often means "always working"**

When you're building your reputation, it's hard to say no. A client in California emails at 10 PM Munich time? You respond. A sensor goes offline on Sunday? You debug it. The flexibility goes both ways.

**2. You're not just building projects, you're building trust**

Technical skills get you the first project. Communication, reliability, and integrity get you the next ten. I've had clients come back after two years for new projects—not because I'm the cheapest, but because they trust me to deliver.

**3. Cash flow is a skill**

Some months you invoice €15,000. Other months, zero. But rent doesn't care about your project pipeline. I learned to keep a 3-month runway and treat "feast months" as preparation for "famine months."

**4. Say no more than yes**

This took me years to learn. Every "yes" to a misaligned project is a "no" to finding better opportunities. I once accepted a project that paid well but involved technologies I wasn't interested in. Six months of misery taught me that life's too short for projects that drain you.

**5. Document everything**

Scope creep is real. "Just a small change" turns into three days of unpaid work. Now I document all requirements upfront and handle changes through formal change requests. It's not being difficult—it's being professional.

**The reality**: Freelancing is running a small business where you happen to be the primary product. It's harder than employment but more rewarding. You control your growth, choose your challenges, and own your outcomes.

Would I go back to a traditional job? Maybe. But not yet. There's still too much to build.
